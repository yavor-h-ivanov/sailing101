Manoeuvres under engine

Nobody is watching when you sail beautifully offshore. Everybody is watching when you berth. A 5-tonne boat with one propeller, no brakes and a keel that wants to pivot is a different animal from a car, and it is learnable.

The physics you must accept

  • Prop walk: in astern the propeller pushes the stern sideways, usually to port with a right-handed propeller. Learn which way yours walks and use it.
  • The boat pivots around a point roughly a third of the way aft from the bow. The bow swings less than you expect; the stern swings more.
  • Steerage needs water flow. At very low speed the rudder does nothing. Short bursts of ahead over the rudder ("kicks") give steering without speed.
  • Wind on the bow blows it off faster than you can correct at low speed. Wind and tide decide your approach, not your preference.
  • Long-keel boats (Finnsailer 35) barely steer in astern; fin keel and saildrive boats (Bavaria 1060) steer well astern.

The standard manoeuvres

  • Coming alongside a pontoon, into and with the wind or tide
  • Leaving a pontoon: springing the bow or stern out
  • Berthing stern-to with lazy lines (Mediterranean and Adriatic)
  • Berthing stern-to or bows-to with your own anchor
  • Box berths with posts (Baltic and German harbours)
  • Picking up a mooring buoy
  • Anchoring: choosing the spot, scope, setting, checking for drag, weighing
  • Rafting up alongside another boat
  • Turning in a narrow space using prop walk
  • Towing and being towed
