Deck hardware and steering

Everything bolted through the deck is a potential leak; everything that moves the rudder is a potential loss of control. Both deserve respect.

Tiller or wheel

  • Tiller (Sadler 32 and many British boats): direct, simple, instant feel, nothing to break, cheap. Takes cockpit space and needs a stronger arm in a blow.
  • Wheel (Moody 33, Bavaria 1060, Finnsailer 35): more comfortable, more familiar, frees the cockpit. Adds cables or rods, a pedestal, a quadrant and several bearings, any of which can fail. Always carry and test the emergency tiller.

Steering systems

  • Cable (wire and chain): the common wheel system. Cables stretch and fray, sheaves seize, the chain jumps if slack.
  • Rod / mechanical: stiffer, more precise, more expensive, found on some German and Scandinavian boats.
  • Hydraulic: usual on motorsailers with two steering positions such as the Finnsailer 35. No feel, but easy to have two helms.

Deck fittings and leaks

Stanchion bases, cleats, tracks, windows, hatches and chainplates all pass through a cored deck. Once the sealant fails, water gets to the core. Signs: staining on the deckhead below, soft spots when you walk, weeping around bolt heads inside lockers.
