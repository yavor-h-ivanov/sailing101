Start here

A one-page cheat sheet for getting into sailing on the kind of boat most people actually start with: a 30–36 ft (9–11 m) glass-fibre cruising yacht built between roughly 1975 and 2005, with an inboard diesel, a masthead or fractional sloop rig, and space for four to six people.

Who this is for

  • You are thinking about buying, chartering or crewing on a boat like a Moody 33, Sadler 32, Bavaria 1060, Gib'Sea 31/33 or Finnsailer 35.
  • You want the vocabulary, the mental model of how each part works, and a realistic list of what goes wrong.
  • You want one place to come back to instead of fifty browser tabs.

How to use this page

  • Scroll or jump. The contents list on the left follows you. Every heading has a # link you can copy and share.
  • Search. Press / and type a term such as cutless, gybe or osmosis.
  • Read the short version first. Bullets and diagrams are the cheat sheet. The More detail blocks are there when you want depth.
  • Component cards follow one pattern: what it is, how it works, positives, negatives, common faults, what to check.
  • Units are metric. Speed in knots (kn), distance in nautical miles (NM), depth in metres, wind in knots and Beaufort force.

A suggested learning path

  1. Anatomy and terminology: learn the names so everything else makes sense.
  2. Sailing fundamentals: points of sail, trim, tacking, gybing, reefing.
  3. Manoeuvres under engine: the part that actually frightens beginners in a marina.
  4. Hull, rig, deck, engine, systems, electronics: what each component does and how it fails.
  5. Navigation and the seas you will sail.
  6. Licences and buying and owning.

The reference fleet in one paragraph

The five boats named above span the whole class. The Moody 33 is a British centre-cockpit cruiser with an aft cabin, offered with fin or twin (bilge) keels. The Sadler 32 is a stiff, sea-kindly British aft-cockpit boat with a foam-filled twin-skin hull. The Bavaria 1060 is a German production cruiser of the early 1990s with a modern fin keel, wheel steering and a roomy interior. The Gib'Sea 31 and 33 are French production cruisers built for Mediterranean charter and family sailing. The Finnsailer 35 is a Finnish motorsailer with a wheelhouse, a long keel and a large engine, built for cold Baltic conditions. Between them they cover fin, bilge and long keels; tiller and wheel; shaft and saildrive; and three very different ideas of what a cruising boat is for. Details and sourced specifications live in the fleet section.
