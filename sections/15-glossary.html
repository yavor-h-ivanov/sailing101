Quick definitions for words used across this page, a curated video library with credits, and books worth owning.

Glossary

The anatomy section defines parts of the boat; this list covers the rest. It is searchable with /.

Apparent wind
The wind you feel on a moving boat: true wind combined with the wind made by your own motion.
Bear away
Turn away from the wind. Opposite of luff up.
Beating
Sailing upwind in a series of tacks.
Broach
An uncontrolled round-up into the wind, usually downwind in strong wind with too much sail.
Chart datum
The reference level from which charted depths are measured, usually close to the lowest astronomical tide.
COLREGs
The International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea.
Dead reckoning
Estimating position from course, speed and time since the last known position.
DSC
Digital Selective Calling: the button on a modern VHF that sends a distress alert with your position.
Fetch
The distance of open water the wind has blown over. More fetch, bigger waves.
Gybe
Turning the stern through the wind so the boom crosses. See Gybing.
Heave to
Stop the boat by backing the headsail and lashing the helm.
In irons
Stuck head to wind with the sails flapping and no steerage.
Knot
One nautical mile per hour. Also a thing you tie.
Lee shore
A shore the wind is blowing onto. Dangerous.
Luff up
Turn towards the wind.
MMSI
Maritime Mobile Service Identity: the nine-digit number identifying your radio and boat.
Nautical mile
1,852 m; one minute of latitude.
Osmosis
Water blistering in a GRP hull. See Hull.
Prop walk
The sideways push of the propeller, most noticeable in astern.
Reef
Reduce sail area.
Scope
The ratio of anchor chain let out to depth of water. More is better.
Spring tide / neap tide
The largest and smallest tidal ranges of the fortnightly cycle.
Tack (verb)
Turn the bow through the wind. Tack (noun): which side the wind is on; also the front-bottom corner of a sail.
Telltale
A ribbon on a sail showing airflow.
TSS
Traffic Separation Scheme: one-way lanes for ships.
Weather helm
The boat's tendency to turn into the wind, felt as a pull on the tiller or wheel.

Reading

  • A general seamanship manual (for example the RYA or Reeds handbooks) for the basics in one place.
  • A boat-buying and surveying guide for this age of GRP boat.
  • A marine diesel maintenance book, ideally the one matching your engine make.
  • Pilot books for each sea you intend to sail.
