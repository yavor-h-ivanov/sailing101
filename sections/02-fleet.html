The reference fleet

Five boats that between them show what a 30–36 ft cruising yacht of 1975–2005 can be. Use them as worked examples; most of what is said about one applies to dozens of similar designs.

Moody 33

A British family cruiser designed by Angus Primrose and built for A. H. Moody & Son. The centre cockpit gives a private aft cabin, unusual at this length, at the cost of a slightly higher centre of gravity and a cockpit that is further from the stern when berthing. Sold with fin or twin bilge keels; the bilge-keel version dries out upright, which matters on the tidal British coast.

  • Strengths: two separate sleeping cabins, protected cockpit, solid GRP hull, large following.
  • Watch for: deck and window leaks, chainplate condition, original engine age, osmosis on early hulls TBC.

Sadler 32

Designed by David Sadler, who also drew the Contessa 32, and built by Sadler Yachts in Poole. Its distinctive feature is a twin-skin hull with closed-cell foam between the skins, marketed as unsinkable and also giving insulation and a quiet interior. Offered with fin, deep fin or bilge keels. A boat with a reputation for good manners in a seaway.

  • Strengths: seaworthy, stiff, well-built, active owners' association.
  • Watch for: water between the skins, engine access, rudder bearings TBC.

Bavaria 1060

An early-1990s production cruiser from Bavaria Yachtbau in Giebelstadt, the era before Bavaria became the volume builder it is today. Wide beam carried aft, a fin keel, wheel steering and a big bright interior. Typical of the modern production formula: more space and speed for the money, less traditional sea-kindliness.

  • Strengths: space, light, easy handling, parts availability, saildrive manoeuvrability.
  • Watch for: saildrive diaphragm age, keel bolt and matrix condition, deck fitting bedding TBC.

Gib'Sea 31 and 33

French production cruisers from Gibert Marine, a yard later absorbed into the Dufour group. Built in numbers for Mediterranean charter fleets and family owners, so many have hard charter miles behind them but also a straightforward, easily maintained specification.

  • Strengths: affordable, simple systems, good cockpit for warm-water sailing.
  • Watch for: charter wear, deck gelcoat cracks, rudder and keel condition after groundings TBC.

Finnsailer 35

A Finnish motorsailer built by Fiskars. A wheelhouse with inside steering, a long keel, a heavy build and a large engine make it a boat for cold, wet seas and long motoring passages rather than for sailing performance. In the Baltic and the North Sea this is a very practical shape.

  • Strengths: shelter, motoring range, directional stability, dries out or takes the ground gracefully.
  • Watch for: wheelhouse window seals, engine bed condition, sailing performance expectations TBC.

The wider class

Dozens of similar boats share the same construction, systems and faults. British: Westerly Fulmar and Konsort, Moody 31 and 346, Sadler 34, Contessa 32, Rival 34. German and Scandinavian: Bavaria 32 and 34, Dehler 34, Hallberg-Rassy 31 and 352, Najad 34, Maxi 33, Albin Ballad and Vega. French: Jeanneau Sun Odyssey 32 and 34, Beneteau First 32 and Oceanis 350, Dufour 31 and 34. Motorsailers: Nauticat 33, Fisher 34, Colvic Watson. When this page says "boats like these", it means that list.
