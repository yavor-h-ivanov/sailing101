The reference fleet

Five boats that between them show what a 30–36 ft cruising yacht of 1969–2005 can be. Use them as worked examples; most of what is said about one applies to dozens of similar designs.

How the figures were checked

Every number below was traced to a published source (the sources are listed under each boat). A value with no mark is given by at least two independent sources. ¹ means one source only. ² means sources disagree and the figure from the most authoritative source (a class association or builder's sheet before a database, a database before a broker) is shown. TBC means not yet found. Builders' brochures rounded generously and boats were modified over long production runs, so treat every figure as ±2%.

Moody 33

A British family cruiser designed by Angus Primrose and built by Marine Projects in Plymouth for A. H. Moody & Son of Swanwick. The centre cockpit gives a private aft cabin, unusual at this length, at the cost of a higher centre of gravity and a helm that is a long way from the stern when berthing. The Mk I (242 built) has a full-width heads compartment between saloon and forecabin and a central hatch into the aft cabin. The Mk II moved the galley aft into an L beside the companionway, put the heads to starboard, offset the aft-cabin hatch to port and squared off the coachroofs. The 33S of 1979–82 is the same hull with an aft cockpit. All are masthead sloops with a skeg-hung rudder and, on most, a cast-iron fin keel bolted to a GRP stub; bilge-keel versions of the later boats exist. The class association's specification sheet describes balsa-cored topsides and a balsa-cored deck with plywood pads under the fittings, so the hull is a sandwich above the waterline, not solid.

  • Strengths: two separate sleeping cabins, a protected cockpit, and a large, active owners' association.
  • Watch for: water in the rudder and osmosis on it; the hull-to-skeg joint, which needed strengthening on some Mk I boats; chainplates sealed under deck plates that leak; cracking around the babystay deck fitting; soft deck around stanchions and fittings where the balsa core has got wet; the earliest 1973 boats with wooden toe rails, which vary in quality; fifty-year-old Thornycroft engines.
Sources and confidence: Moody 33
  • Dimensions, displacement, ballast, sail area: sailboatdata Mk I, Mk II, 33S; boats.com review of the Moody 33, 36 and 39.
  • Production years and numbers: Moody Owners Association archive, Mk I (September 1973 to September 1978, 242 built), Mk II (September 1978 to June 1981, 121 built) and 33S (September 1979 to February 1982, 54 built). The Yachtsnet archive gives 1973–76 and 1976–81 instead; the class archive is taken as authoritative.
  • Construction: Moody Owners Association specification sheet for the Moody 33 (balsa-cored topsides and deck, plywood pads under fittings). A forum post says the opposite; the spec sheet wins.
  • Mk I vs Mk II differences: Moody owners' forum thread.
  • Keel material: brokers' listings describe a cast-iron fin bolted to a reinforced GRP stub; one database says lead. Cast iron is the more likely for a British production boat of 1973.
  • Engine: owners' thread on the Thornycroft T90. Database entries quoting "Thornycroft-Perkins" or a T80 appear to be garbled or to describe re-engined boats.
  • Known issues: YBW forum thread on the Moody 33/333/33S.

Sadler 32

Designed by David Sadler as a beamier, roomier development of his Contessa 32, and built by Sadler Yachts in Poole from 1979 to 1989, with about 300 launched and a few more built from the moulds afterwards ¹. The hull is conventional solid GRP with a balsa-cored deck; it is the Sadler 26, 29 and 34 that have the foam-filled twin-skin "unsinkable" construction, and several marque pages wrongly credit the 32 with it (owners and Yachting Monthly's used-boat review are clear that it is a solid laminate). Offered with a deep fin, a shoal fin, twin bilge keels or a lifting centreboard, always with a skeg-hung rudder and, on nearly all boats, a tiller. Ballast ratio around 44% makes it stiff; the Contessa ancestry makes it well mannered at sea.

  • Strengths: seaworthy, stiff, quick for a cruiser, a strong owners' association, and a hull that surveyors like.
  • Watch for: soft side decks where the balsa core has got wet; osmosis on many hulls; leaks and delamination around the chainplates; window leaks; interiors that vary because some boats were home-completed from kits; three generations of engine (Watermota, Bukh, Volvo) with very different parts availability.
Sources and confidence: Sadler 32
  • Dimensions and weights: sailboatdata, go-sail.co.uk. Deep-fin draught is quoted as 1.60, 1.68 and 1.70 m by different sources.
  • History, keel options, engines: Lucas Yachting and its engine installation note; Darglow's brief history of Sadler Yachts.
  • Reviews: Yachting Monthly and its 2026 used-boat review.
  • Construction (solid GRP, not twin-skin) and known issues: owners on the YBW forum, thread 449337, thread 518235, thread 384695.

Bavaria 1060

A mid-1980s production cruiser from Bavaria Yachtbau in Giebelstadt, designed by Axel Mohnhaupt in the years before Bavaria became the volume builder it is today. A fin keel with a shallow option, a spade rudder, a masthead rig and, in the usual two-cabin version, an aft cabin tucked under the cockpit to port ¹. The figures say what kind of boat it is: at about 5,400 kg on an 8.6 m waterline with a ballast ratio near 41%, it is a moderately heavy, conventionally built mid-1980s cruiser, not the light, wide boat the Bavaria name suggests today.

  • Strengths: space and headroom below for its age (about 1.90 m in the saloon ¹), easy handling, a moderate, seakindly displacement.
  • Watch for: a forty-year-old Volvo Penta MD11C or MD17, for which spares are now scarce, and its heat exchanger and exhaust elbow; osmosis, to which 1980s German laminates are not immune; hull-to-deck joint and window seals; chainplate seepage; keel bolts, simply because of their age. The faults Bavaria owners' forums are full of (water in the rudder, the "smile" at the keel stub, rudder bearings, saildrive diaphragms) are mostly reported on 1997–2010 boats of a different hull design and should not be assumed here. Nothing specific to the 1060 has been found; production numbers and the end of production are still unknown, and the owners' network is oriented to later boats.
Sources and confidence: Bavaria 1060
  • Dimensions: sailboatdata, sailingtheweb, yachtdatabase. The waterline length is given as 8.59 m and 9.00 m by different sources.
  • Years: sailboatdata says first built 1985; J.D. Power lists 1983, 1984 and 1986 model years of a "Bavaria 1060/SL"; the German magazine YACHT tested the boat in issue 2/85 (not online; the test is the source to chase for the sail-area basis and the ballast).
  • Engine spares situation: ASAP Supplies, Volvo Penta MD-series parts.
  • Ballast, keel options, engine and layout: Listings Port (single source; treat with care).
  • Known issues: Bavaria Owners Association forum, keel problems and rudder problems.

Gib'Sea 31 and 33

French production cruisers from Gibert Marine of Marans, near La Rochelle, a yard later absorbed into the Dufour group. The Gib'Sea 31 (1979–84, 239 built) is a Joubert-Nivelt design with a fin keel, an aft cockpit and a private double aft cabin, light at 3,700 kg. Built in numbers for charter fleets and family owners, so many have hard miles behind them but also a straightforward specification. A "31 DL" version (dériveur lesté, a ballasted centreboarder) drew about 1.10 m with the board up. The later Gib'Sea 312 (1988–95, 86 built, credited to Rob Humphreys by one source) is a different design of similar size, not a Mk II.

Which Gib'Sea 33?

Three quite different boats carry the name. (a) The original Gib'Sea 33 by Philippe Harlé, built 1973/74–80: 9.89 m long, 3.46 m beam, 1.71 m draught, about 5,180 kg, 112 built. (b) A late-1980s to mid-1990s Gib'Sea 33 by Joubert-Nivelt: about 9.75 × 3.44 × 1.87 m (figures from a 1994 boat's listing), with a Yanmar 3GMF (20 hp) in that example. (c) The 2002 Gib'Sea 33 by J&J Design and Olivier Poncin, built by Dufour: 9.98 m long, 8.89 m waterline, 3.48 m beam, 1.35 m draught, 5,171 kg. Only the 31 is in the comparison table; the three 33s are listed here so that a buyer reading an advert knows which boat is meant.

  • Strengths: affordable, simple systems, a good cockpit for warm-water sailing, an active UK owners' association.
  • Watch for: charter wear, deck gelcoat crazing, rudder and keel condition after groundings. No model-specific fault list has been found yet.
Sources and confidence: Gib'Sea 31 and 33
  • Gib'Sea 31: bateaux.com fiche technique (3,700 kg is the light-ship figure), sailingtheweb, sailboatdata; ballast 1,200 kg, solid GRP hull and the 31 DL draughts from ouest-atlantis. Sail area, rudder and steering not found.
  • Gib'Sea 312: sailboatdata, bateaux.com.
  • Gib'Sea 33 generations: bateaux.com (1974 boat), sailboatdata 33-1 and 33-2, Yachtsnet archive (Joubert-Nivelt boat), boats.com review of the 2002 boat.
  • Owners: The Gib'Sea Association.

Finnsailer 35

A Finnish motorsailer built by the yacht division of Oy Fiskars Ab in Turku from 1969 into the 1980s, to lines derived from a Baltic pilot boat and built under Lloyd's Register survey. A wheelhouse with a second steering position and engine controls inside, a long keel, a heavy hand-laid hull and a 75 hp Perkins make it a boat for cold, wet seas and long motoring passages rather than for sailing performance. Yachting Monthly placed it "well towards the motor end of the motor-sailer spectrum" with reasonable performance off the wind. In the Baltic and the North Sea this is a very practical shape.

  • Strengths: shelter, motoring range (about 300 litres of fuel), directional stability, and a keel that takes the ground without drama.
  • Watch for: osmosis (at least one hull has been peeled); ageing teak decks; wheelhouse window seals; and the long keel's reluctance to steer in astern, which makes marina manoeuvres a skill in themselves. Do not expect to point high or to tack quickly.
Sources and confidence: Finnsailer 35
  • Dimensions: Yachting Monthly profile, sailboatdata, sailboat-cruising.com. Displacement is given as 6,214 kg by most sources and 7,257 kg by one.
  • Designer: databases name Turun Veneveistämö, which was the Turku yard that Fiskars took over, so this is "designed in-house" rather than a named designer; one source names Hans Groop, who is documented for the Finnsailer 34 rather than the 35.
  • Builder, Lloyd's survey, construction: a 1969 example listed by Calibre Yachts; sailboat.guide.
  • Engine, tankage, steering: yachtdatabase, Listings Port.
  • Owner material: finnsailer35.wordpress.com, finnsailer.dk specifications.

The wider class

Dozens of similar boats share the same construction, systems and faults. British: Westerly Fulmar, Discus and Seahawk, Moody 31, 333, 336 and 346, Sadler 34, Contessa 32, Rival 34, Rustler 31. German, Dutch and Nordic: Bavaria 32 and 34, Dehler 34, Hallberg-Rassy Monsun 31 and 352, Najad 34, Maxi 33, Albin Ballad, Finngulf 33, Contest 33. French: Jeanneau Sun Odyssey 32 and 34, Beneteau First 32 (the 1980s Berret design) and Oceanis 350, Dufour 31 and 34. Motorsailers: Nauticat 33 and 331, Fisher 34, Colvic Watson 32 and 35. When this page says "boats like these", it means that list.
