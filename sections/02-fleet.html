The reference fleet

Five boats that between them show what a 30–36 ft cruising yacht of 1969–2005 can be. Use them as worked examples; most of what is said about one applies to dozens of similar designs.

How the figures were checked

Every number below was traced to a published source (the sources are listed under each boat). A value with no mark is given by at least two independent sources. ¹ means one source only. ² means sources disagree and the most widely quoted figure is shown. TBC means not yet found. Builders' brochures rounded generously and boats were modified over long production runs, so treat every figure as ±2%.

Moody 33

A British family cruiser designed by Angus Primrose and built by Marine Projects in Plymouth for A. H. Moody & Son of Swanwick. The centre cockpit gives a private aft cabin, unusual at this length, at the cost of a higher centre of gravity and a helm that is a long way from the stern when berthing. The Mk I (242 built) has a full-width heads compartment between saloon and forecabin and a central hatch into the aft cabin. The Mk II moved the galley aft into an L beside the companionway, put the heads to starboard, offset the aft-cabin hatch to port and squared off the coachroofs. The 33S of 1979–82 is the same hull with an aft cockpit. All are masthead sloops with a skeg-hung rudder and, on most, a cast-iron fin keel bolted to a GRP stub; bilge-keel versions of the later boats exist.

  • Strengths: two separate sleeping cabins, a protected cockpit, a solid GRP hull and deck with no balsa core, and a large, active owners' association.
  • Watch for: water in the rudder and osmosis on it; the hull-to-skeg joint, which needed strengthening on some Mk I boats; chainplates sealed under deck plates that leak; cracking around the babystay deck fitting; the earliest 1973 boats with wooden toe rails, which vary in quality; forty-year-old Thornycroft engines.
Sources and confidence: Moody 33
  • Dimensions, displacement, ballast, sail area: sailboatdata Mk I, Mk II, 33S; boats.com review of the Moody 33, 36 and 39.
  • Production years and numbers: Moody Owners Association archive (Mk I introduced September 1973, ceased September 1978; 242 built), Yachtsnet archive (Mk I 1973–76, Mk II 1976–81). The two disagree on the Mk I / Mk II boundary; production of the two versions probably overlapped.
  • Mk I vs Mk II differences: Moody owners' forum thread.
  • Keel material: brokers' listings describe a cast-iron fin bolted to a reinforced GRP stub; one database says lead. Cast iron is the more likely for a British production boat of 1973.
  • Engine: owners' thread on the Thornycroft T90; database entries quoting "Thornycroft-Perkins 35 hp".
  • Known issues: YBW forum thread on the Moody 33/333/33S.

Sadler 32

Designed by David Sadler as a beamier, roomier development of his Contessa 32, and built by Sadler Yachts in Poole from 1979 to 1989, with about 300 launched and a further dozen built from the moulds on the East Coast into the mid-1990s. The hull is conventional solid GRP; it is the Sadler 26, 29 and 34 that have the foam-filled twin-skin "unsinkable" construction, and the 32 is often wrongly credited with it. Offered with a deep fin, a shoal fin, twin bilge keels or a lifting centreboard, always with a skeg-hung rudder and, on nearly all boats, a tiller. Ballast ratio around 44% makes it stiff; the Contessa ancestry makes it well mannered at sea.

  • Strengths: seaworthy, stiff, quick for a cruiser, a strong owners' association, and a hull that surveyors like.
  • Watch for: soft side decks where the balsa core has got wet; osmosis on many hulls; leaks and delamination around the chainplates; window leaks; interiors that vary because some boats were home-completed from kits; three generations of engine (Watermota, Bukh, Volvo) with very different parts availability.
Sources and confidence: Sadler 32
  • Dimensions and weights: sailboatdata, go-sail.co.uk. Deep-fin draught is quoted as 1.60, 1.68 and 1.70 m by different sources.
  • History, keel options, engines: Lucas Yachting and its engine installation note; Darglow's brief history of Sadler Yachts.
  • Reviews: Yachting Monthly and its 2026 used-boat review.
  • Construction (solid GRP, not twin-skin) and known issues: owners on the YBW forum, thread 449337, thread 518235, thread 384695.

Bavaria 1060

A mid-1980s production cruiser from Bavaria Yachtbau in Giebelstadt, designed by Axel Mohnhaupt in the years before Bavaria became the volume builder it is today. A fin keel with a shallow option, a spade rudder, a masthead rig and a two-cabin interior with the aft cabin tucked under the cockpit to port. Typical of the German production formula of its day: more space and speed for the money than the British boats, with less traditional sea-kindliness.

  • Strengths: space and light below, easy handling, parts availability through a large owners' network.
  • Watch for: the issues Bavaria owners report across the range: water in the rudder, the "smile" crack at the keel-to-stub joint (check the keel bolt torque), rudder-bearing wear and loose top-bearing bolts, and on saildrive boats the diaphragm replacement interval. Nothing specific to the 1060 has been found; production numbers and the end of production are still unknown.
Sources and confidence: Bavaria 1060
  • Dimensions: sailboatdata, sailingtheweb, yachtdatabase. The waterline length is given as 8.59 m and 9.00 m by different sources.
  • Years: sailboatdata says first built 1985; J.D. Power lists 1983, 1984 and 1986 model years of a "Bavaria 1060/SL"; the German magazine YACHT tested the boat in issue 2/85.
  • Ballast, keel options, engine and layout: Listings Port (single source; treat with care).
  • Known issues: Bavaria Owners Association forum, keel problems and rudder problems.

Gib'Sea 31 and 33

French production cruisers from Gibert Marine of Marans, near La Rochelle, a yard later absorbed into the Dufour group. The Gib'Sea 31 (1979–84, 239 built) is a Joubert-Nivelt design with a fin keel, an aft cockpit and a private double aft cabin, light at 3,700 kg. Built in numbers for charter fleets and family owners, so many have hard miles behind them but also a straightforward specification. The later Gib'Sea 312 (1988–95, 86 built) is a different, smaller design, not a Mk II.

Which Gib'Sea 33?

Three quite different boats carry the name. (a) The original 1974–80 Gib'Sea 33 by Philippe Harlé: 9.89 m long, 3.46 m beam, 1.71 m draught, about 5,180 kg, 112 built. (b) A late-1980s to mid-1990s Gib'Sea 33 by Joubert-Nivelt: about 9.75 × 3.44 × 1.87 m, typically with a Yanmar 3GMF 27 hp. (c) The 2002 Gib'Sea 33 by J&J Design and Olivier Poncin, built by Dufour: 9.98 m long, 8.89 m waterline, 3.48 m beam, 1.35 m draught, 5,171 kg. This page will settle on one once the owner says which is meant; the comparison table above therefore lists only the 31 for now.

  • Strengths: affordable, simple systems, a good cockpit for warm-water sailing, an active UK owners' association.
  • Watch for: charter wear, deck gelcoat crazing, rudder and keel condition after groundings. No model-specific fault list has been found yet.
Sources and confidence: Gib'Sea 31 and 33
  • Gib'Sea 31: bateaux.com fiche technique, sailingtheweb, sailboatdata. Ballast, sail area, rudder and steering not found. A "31 DL" variant appears in listings, probably a ballasted centreboard version (not verified).
  • Gib'Sea 312: sailboatdata, bateaux.com.
  • Gib'Sea 33 generations: bateaux.com (1974 boat), sailboatdata 33-1 and 33-2, Yachtsnet archive (Joubert-Nivelt boat), boats.com review of the 2002 boat.
  • Owners: The Gib'Sea Association.

Finnsailer 35

A Finnish motorsailer built by the yacht division of Oy Fiskars Ab in Turku from 1969 into the 1980s, to lines derived from a Baltic pilot boat and built under Lloyd's Register survey. A wheelhouse with a second steering position and engine controls inside, a long keel, a heavy hand-laid hull and a 75 hp Perkins make it a boat for cold, wet seas and long motoring passages rather than for sailing performance. Yachting Monthly placed it "well towards the motor end of the motor-sailer spectrum" with reasonable performance off the wind. In the Baltic and the North Sea this is a very practical shape.

  • Strengths: shelter, motoring range (about 300 litres of fuel), directional stability, and a keel that takes the ground without drama.
  • Watch for: osmosis (at least one hull has been peeled); ageing teak decks; wheelhouse window seals; and the long keel's reluctance to steer in astern, which makes marina manoeuvres a skill in themselves. Do not expect to point high or to tack quickly.
Sources and confidence: Finnsailer 35
  • Dimensions: Yachting Monthly profile, sailboatdata, sailboat-cruising.com. Displacement is given as 6,214 kg by most sources and 7,257 kg by one.
  • Designer: most databases credit the Turku boatyard's own office; one source names Hans Groop, who is documented for the Finnsailer 29 and 34 rather than the 35.
  • Builder, Lloyd's survey, construction: a 1969 example listed by Calibre Yachts; sailboat.guide.
  • Engine, tankage, steering: yachtdatabase, Listings Port.
  • Owner material: finnsailer35.wordpress.com.

The wider class

Dozens of similar boats share the same construction, systems and faults. British: Westerly Fulmar and Konsort, Moody 31 and 346, Sadler 34, Contessa 32, Rival 34. German and Scandinavian: Bavaria 32 and 34, Dehler 34, Hallberg-Rassy 31 and 352, Najad 34, Maxi 33, Albin Ballad and Vega. French: Jeanneau Sun Odyssey 32 and 34, Beneteau First 32 and Oceanis 350, Dufour 31 and 34. Motorsailers: Nauticat 33, Fisher 34, Colvic Watson. When this page says "boats like these", it means that list.
