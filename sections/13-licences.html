Licences and qualifications

Whether you legally need a licence depends on the flag of the boat, the country whose waters you are in, and sometimes the size of the engine. Whether you should get trained does not depend on anything.

Rules change and vary by flag state

What follows is a map of the landscape, not legal advice. Every entry is TBC against the current official source before you rely on it.

The RYA ladder (UK, recognised widely)

  1. Start Yachting / Competent Crew: being useful on board.
  2. Day Skipper (theory and practical): skippering a yacht on short passages by day in familiar waters. The usual first target for owners of this class.
  3. Coastal Skipper / Yachtmaster Coastal: longer passages, night, more demanding conditions.
  4. Yachtmaster Offshore and Ocean: the professional-grade certificates.

Supporting courses: VHF Short Range Certificate (required to operate a marine VHF radio), Sea Survival, First Aid, Diesel Engine, Radar.

International Certificate of Competence (ICC)

Issued under UNECE Resolution 40. It is the document most Mediterranean, Adriatic and inland-waterway authorities want to see from a visiting skipper. Obtainable through the RYA (for eligible people) on the basis of Day Skipper or an assessment, and from national authorities in countries that have adopted the resolution. The CEVNI endorsement covers European inland waterways.

National schemes for the seas covered here

  • Germany: Sportbootführerschein See (SBF See) for coastal waters, then SKS, SSS and SHS. Required for German-flagged boats and expected on the German coast TBC.
  • Croatia: a skipper's licence recognised by the Croatian authorities plus a VHF certificate is required to charter or to operate a boat in Croatian waters TBC.
  • Italy: Patente nautica required beyond a set distance from the coast or above a set engine power for Italian-flagged boats TBC.
  • Greece, Turkey: a skipper's certificate is required to charter; a co-skipper or crew declaration is often needed too TBC.
  • Bulgaria and Romania (Black Sea): national certificates of competence for pleasure craft; recognition of foreign certificates TBC.
  • France, Spain: licence requirements for nationally flagged boats; visiting foreign-flag skippers are generally judged by flag-state rules TBC.
  • Netherlands: Klein Vaarbewijs for inland waters and certain sizes; sea use rules differ TBC.
  • Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Norway: historically no licence for sailing yachts of this size, with exceptions for fast motorboats; radio licence still required TBC.
  • United Kingdom: no licence required for pleasure sailing on a UK-flagged boat; VHF operator licence and ship radio licence required.

The boat's own papers

  • Registration (for the UK, Part 1 or Part 3 SSR); proof of ownership; VAT status evidence.
  • Ship radio licence and MMSI; insurance with third-party cover (mandatory in many countries).
  • Safety equipment lists that some countries prescribe by distance from shore.
