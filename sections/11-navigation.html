Navigation and passage planning

Knowing where you are, where you are going, what is in the way and when the tide and weather will let you. The plotter does the arithmetic; you still have to do the thinking.

Position and direction

  • Latitude and longitude; one minute of latitude is one nautical mile (1,852 m).
  • True, magnetic and compass north; variation and deviation.
  • Course, heading, course over ground, track. Speed through water vs speed over ground.
  • Charts: paper, raster and vector electronic charts; chart datum; symbols and abbreviations.

Tides and currents

  • Springs and neaps, tidal range and tidal streams. Essential in the Channel and North Sea; nearly absent in the Mediterranean, Black Sea and Baltic.
  • Tide tables, tidal curves, the rule of twelfths, tidal stream atlases and tidal diamonds.
  • Secondary ports and height of tide calculations, for the days you want to cross a bar or dry out.

Collision regulations (COLREGs)

  • Lookout, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision.
  • Sailing vessels: port gives way to starboard; windward gives way to leeward; overtaking vessel keeps clear.
  • Power gives way to sail, except when sail is overtaking, in narrow channels, in traffic separation schemes, and to vessels restricted in their ability to manoeuvre, fishing or not under command.
  • Lights and shapes; sound signals; traffic separation schemes.

Buoyage and lights

  • IALA region A (all the waters on this page): red to port when entering harbour.
  • Lateral, cardinal, isolated danger, safe water and special marks.
  • Light characteristics: how to read "Fl(2) 10s" on a chart.

Weather

  • Synoptic charts, fronts, and what a falling barometer means.
  • Beaufort scale, sea state, and what your boat and crew can actually handle.
  • Forecast sources per sea: GRIB files, national met services, VHF and NAVTEX broadcasts.
  • Local wind systems: sea breezes, katabatic winds, the named winds of each sea.

Passage planning

The SOLAS V requirement in plain terms: know the weather, the tides, the dangers, the ports of refuge, the crew's limits and the boat's state before leaving. Write it down. Tell someone.
