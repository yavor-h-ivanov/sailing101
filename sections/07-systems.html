Boat systems

Electricity, water, gas, heating, fuel and waste. None of it is complicated on a boat this size, but all of it lives in a damp, moving, salty box.

Electrical

  • 12 V DC from a house battery bank and a separate engine start battery, charged by the alternator, shore power charger and increasingly solar.
  • Lead-acid (flooded, AGM or gel) is still normal on this class. Lithium is spreading but needs a properly designed installation.
  • Corroded terminals, undersized cable, missing fuses and thirty years of previous owners' additions are the standard state of affairs.

Water

  • Fresh water tanks (100–300 litres typical TBC) with a pressure pump or foot pumps.
  • Hot water from an engine-heated calorifier, also with an immersion heater on shore power.
  • Sea water for the heads, deck wash and sometimes a galley pump.

Gas

  • Butane or propane bottles in a sealed locker that drains overboard, feeding a gimballed cooker through a regulator and a shut-off.
  • Bottle fittings differ by country: a real problem when cruising between the seas covered here.

Heating and ventilation

  • Diesel blown-air heaters (Eberspächer, Webasto) or drip-feed diesel stoves (Refleks) for the Baltic and North Sea. Little heating on Mediterranean boats.
  • Ventilation prevents condensation and mildew: dorade vents, mushroom vents, opening portlights.

Heads and waste

  • Manual sea-water toilet pumping to a holding tank or directly overboard. Holding tanks are mandatory in many of the waters covered here.
  • Joker valves, pump seals and calcified pipes are the recurring faults.
