Electronics

Instruments tell you where you are, how deep it is, how fast the wind blows and who else is out there. Old boats carry every generation of them at once.

The core set

  • Depth sounder: the one instrument you cannot do without.
  • Speed log (paddle wheel through the hull) and GPS speed: one gives speed through the water, the other over the ground. The difference is the current.
  • Wind instrument: masthead vane and cups, giving apparent wind; with the log, true wind.
  • VHF radio with DSC: for safety, harbours and other boats. Requires an operator licence (see Licences).
  • Chartplotter or tablet with charts: modern navigation. Paper charts are still the backup that never crashes.
  • AIS receiver or transponder: shows ships around you and, with a transponder, shows you to them.
  • Autopilot: the most useful crew member on a short-handed boat.

How they talk to each other

  • NMEA 0183: the old serial standard, one talker to several listeners. Found on most boats of this era.
  • NMEA 2000: the modern CAN-bus network with a backbone and drop cables. Raymarine SeaTalkNG is a variant with its own connectors.
  • Wi-Fi bridges put the data on a phone or tablet.
